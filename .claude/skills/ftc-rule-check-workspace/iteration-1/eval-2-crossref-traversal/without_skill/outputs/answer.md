Good question to nail down before you commit to an arm design — expansion limits are one of the most common inspection/penalty issues.

Here's how it works in FTC, but read the important caveat at the bottom first.

## The two things that get measured

**1. Starting size (pre-match):**
Your robot has to fit inside an 18 in. x 18 in. x 18 in. sizing cube at the start of the match. This is checked at inspection with no power applied and everything in its most-collapsed (smallest) legal starting configuration. This 18x18x18 number has been the standard starting envelope for many seasons and is very stable year to year.

**2. Expansion (once the match starts):**
Once the match is underway you ARE allowed to expand past that 18-inch cube — that's normal and expected (arms, slides, intakes all do it). But there is a defined limit on how far, and that limit is **set per season in the game manual**. It is not "expand as much as you want."

## What the expansion limit typically looks like

Across recent seasons the expansion rule has usually taken one of these forms (sometimes combined):

- A **maximum horizontal expansion** — e.g., your robot may not extend more than a set number of inches beyond its frame perimeter in a given direction, or must fit within a defined expanded footprint (a common figure in recent seasons has been on the order of a ~20 in. horizontal extension limit, but the exact value changes).
- A **maximum height** during the match in some seasons.
- Sometimes a rule that you can only expand in a limited number of directions, or that total expansion must fit within a specified boundary/cylinder.

For an arm specifically, the horizontal reach is what usually bites teams — a long extending arm can blow past the horizontal expansion cap even if the rest of the robot is fine.

## What this means for your arm

1. Find your season's Robot Construction / sizing rule in the current Competition Manual (Game Manual Part 1). Look for the rule that governs "expansion" or "maximum size during the match" — it's right next to the 18x18x18 starting-size rule.
2. Note the exact horizontal expansion number and whether it's measured from the frame perimeter or from the field, and whether it's per-direction or a total footprint.
3. Design your arm so that at **full extension** it still stays inside that limit. Test it physically at competition-legal max, not just on CAD.
4. If you can exceed the limit mechanically, add a hard stop (mechanical or software limit) so you can't accidentally over-extend in a match — over-expansion is typically a MINOR/MAJOR penalty and can draw a card if repeated.

## Important caveat (please verify)

I've given you the structure from memory, and the 18x18x18 **starting** size is reliable. But the **exact expansion allowance — the number of inches, the direction rules, and the rule number — changes every season**, and I can't give you the current season's precise value with confidence from memory.
