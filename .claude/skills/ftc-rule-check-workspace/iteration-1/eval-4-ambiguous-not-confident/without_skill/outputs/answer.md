Short version: I would not do this, and I'm fairly confident the manual disallows it — but you should verify the exact rule before trusting me on the number.

Here's my reasoning from what I remember of the FTC Competition Manual:

- The manual has a general rule that robots may not damage, mark, or leave residue on the playing field tiles or on other robots. Silicone traction spray is exactly the kind of thing that gets flagged here — it can transfer off your wheels onto the foam tiles, making them slick for every other robot on the field. That "you can't alter the field surface for others" principle is a very consistent theme across FTC seasons.
- There's also traditionally language restricting liquids, gels, and lubricants on the robot, with dry lubricants sometimes treated differently from wet/sprayed ones. A wet silicone spray applied to a contact surface is the risky category.

So my honest read: **very likely illegal**, and even if some narrow version were technically allowed, an inspector could reasonably fail you for it, and it's the kind of thing that draws a protest from other teams.

Important caveat: I do not have the current season's manual in front of me, and I don't want to give you a rule number I'm not sure about. FTC rule numbers (the R-series robot rules and the game-specific rules) change season to season, and quoting the wrong one is worse than quoting none. Please confirm before you rely on this.

What I'd suggest:
1. Search the current Competition Manual (PDF, Ctrl-F) for "residue", "damage", "playing field", "lubricant", and "traction". The relevant robot rules are in the Robot Construction Rules section (the "R" rules).
2. If it's still ambiguous after that, post it to the official FTC Q&A forum for your season — that's the only answer an inspector is bound by.
3. Practically: for extra grip on mecanum wheels, teams usually stay legal by choosing wheels with better durometer/tread rather than spraying anything. That avoids the whole question.

Bottom line: I'd treat spraying silicone traction spray on your wheels as not allowed and not risk it, but verify the specific rule in the current manual rather than taking my word for the exact citation.
